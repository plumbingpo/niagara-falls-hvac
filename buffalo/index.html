A few minutes of caution before we arrive can head off a much larger repair. An iced-over AC should be turned off at the thermostat with the fan set to "on", which lets the coil thaw safely. Running it frozen can flood the drain pan and damage the compressor. A short-cycling furnace, or any burning smell, means it's time to shut the unit off at its service switch and call us immediately.

How Buffalo Homeowners Can Extend HVAC Life

The best way to avoid a mid-season breakdown is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners replace filters every one to three months, keep shrubs and grass trimmed two feet back from the outdoor condenser, rinse the condenser coil each spring, and schedule a professional tune-up twice a year, cooling in spring and heating in fall, so small problems get caught while they're still cheap.

Age matters too. Most central air conditioners run 12 to 15 years, furnaces 15 to 20, and heat pumps 10 to 15 with regular service. If your system is past that window and showing symptoms, such as climbing utility bills, rooms that never feel comfortable, or repairs stacking up season after season, a planned replacement is nearly always less expensive and much easier on you than an emergency one during the first heat wave of summer.

If comfort problems do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A weak capacitor is a quick fix. The same capacitor left alone for a season can take the compressor with it and turn a minor repair into a full system replacement. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and miserable nights.
